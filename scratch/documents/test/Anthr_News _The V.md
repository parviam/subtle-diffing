**A Day in the Life at Anthropic’s San Francisco HQ**  
*By Maya Rao, The Verge*  
*October 28, 2025 — San Francisco*  

When you step through the glass doors of Anthropic’s 12‑story campus on Mission Street, the first thing you notice isn’t the sleek, minimalist design or the wall‑sized screens displaying live model outputs—it’s the hum of conversation. Teams of engineers, researchers, and ethicists move between white‑board‑lined corridors, coffee mugs in hand, debating everything from prompt engineering to the moral implications of “Claude”‑style assistants.  

Anthropic’s story began in 2021, when former OpenAI employees Daniela Amodei and Dario Amodei left the San Francisco Bay Area’s most famous AI lab to start their own venture. Their goal was simple yet ambitious: build “aligned, interpretable AI” that could be trusted in high‑stakes settings. That founding narrative still shapes the company’s culture, and it’s evident from the moment you arrive.  

### Morning: A Structured Start  

The day officially kicks off at 8:30 a.m. with a brief “stand‑up” in the central atrium. Each team shares a quick status update, but the real focus is on safety checkpoints. “We have a ‘red‑team’ rotation that started when the Amodei siblings were still setting the tone,” says Dr. Priya Natarajan, a senior research scientist. “Every morning we ask, ‘Is there a scenario where today’s work could unintentionally cause harm?’ It’s a habit we inherited from the founders’ early emphasis on pre‑emptive risk assessment.”  

After the stand‑up, employees drift to the kitchen—an airy space with a wall of espresso machines and a rotating selection of pastries from a local bakery. The coffee is strong, the conversation stronger. “I love that the founders made it clear that we should treat each other like collaborators, not competitors,” notes Alex Kim, a machine‑learning engineer. “It’s why you’ll see people from the language‑model team chatting with the policy group over a latte.”  

### Midday: Collaboration and Community  

Lunch is a communal affair. The cafeteria offers a “build‑your‑own‑bowl” concept, but the real draw is the weekly “Ethics Hour” that takes place on the second floor. Today’s speaker is Dr. Maya Patel, who leads Anthropic’s external partnerships. She’s discussing the latest research on “interpretability metrics” and how they tie back to the original vision laid out by Daniela and Dario.  

“The Amodei founders believed that transparency isn’t a feature; it’s a foundation,” Patel explains. “Everything we do—from the way we label training data to how we expose model internals to users—stems from that belief.”  

### Afternoon: Building Claude  

Post‑lunch, the office buzzes with a live demo of Claude 3.5, the newest iteration of Anthropic’s flagship assistant. Engineers gather around a large screen, feeding the model a series of complex, multi‑turn prompts. The model not only answers accurately but also provides a confidence score and a brief rationale—an explicit nod to the company’s alignment goals.  

“Seeing Claude explain *why* it chose a particular answer is exactly the kind of transparency the founders wanted,” says Maya Rao, a product manager. “It’s a small but powerful reminder that we’re building tools that can be audited, not just black boxes.”  

### Evening: Reflections on a Founding Legacy  

As the sun sets over the Bay, the office gradually empties. A few night‑owl researchers linger, polishing code or drafting policy briefs. The building’s rooftop garden, a quiet oasis of native California plants, offers a place to unwind and reflect.  

“The Amodei siblings left OpenAI because they felt the field needed a different kind of guardrail,” says Dr. Natarajan, looking out over the city lights. “That guardrail is still the backbone of our day‑to‑day decisions. It’s why we spend as much time on safety reviews as we do on model scaling.”  

Anthropic’s headquarters feels less like a traditional tech office and more like a living laboratory—one where the founding ethos of 2021 is not a relic but a daily practice. For a company that set out to make AI safer from the start, that continuity is both its compass and its culture.

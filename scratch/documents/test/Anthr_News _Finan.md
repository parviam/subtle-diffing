**Venture capital shifts: AI startups draw record funding as investors chase the next frontier**  
*By Emma Clarke, FT Technology Correspondent*  
*28 October 2025, London*  

U.S. venture capital activity has entered a new phase, with a pronounced tilt toward late‑stage financing for artificial‑intelligence firms. According to data from PitchBook, U.S. VC firms deployed $215 billion in 2024, a 12 % rise on the previous year, and AI‑related deals accounted for more than a third of that total. The surge reflects both the maturation of generative‑AI technologies and a growing appetite among Silicon Valley investors for “scale‑up” opportunities that promise rapid commercialisation.

At the centre of this trend is Anthropic, the AI research lab founded in 2021 by former OpenAI employees Daniela Amodei and Dario Amodei. The sibling duo, who left OpenAI to build a safety‑first approach to large language models, have now secured a record‑breaking $450 million Series C round. The financing, led by a consortium of Silicon Valley stalwarts—including Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia Capital and a new fund from Alphabet’s venture arm—pushes Anthropic’s valuation to just over $5 billion, making it the largest single round ever raised by an AI start‑up in the United States.

The size of the round is noteworthy not only for its headline figure but also for the composition of the investors. “We see Anthropic as a strategic partner in the broader AI ecosystem,” said Maya Patel, partner at Andreessen Horowitz, in a statement to the press. “Their emphasis on alignment and safety aligns with the long‑term risk‑adjusted returns we are targeting.”

Anthropic’s fundraising success follows a string of high‑profile AI deals. OpenAI closed a $1 billion round in early 2024, while Stability AI raised $300 million later that year. Yet, Anthropic’s round stands out for its focus on later‑stage capital, signalling a shift away from the early‑stage “seed‑to‑Series B” frenzy that characterised the AI boom of 2022‑23.

Venture capitalists are increasingly favouring companies that have moved beyond proof‑of‑concept to demonstrable product‑market fit. “The market is maturing,” observed Carlos Mendes, senior analyst at CB Insights. “Investors are now looking for firms that can translate cutting‑edge research into revenue streams, and Anthropic’s latest round is a textbook example of that evolution.”

The influx of capital is also reshaping the competitive dynamics among AI firms. With deep pockets, Anthropic plans to accelerate the development of its Claude series of chatbots, expand its enterprise offerings, and double down on safety research. Competitors are likely to respond with their own fundraising pushes, potentially intensifying a bidding war for talent and compute resources.

Looking ahead, the venture community expects AI to remain a dominant theme throughout 2026, but with a more measured approach to valuation and a greater emphasis on sustainable growth. As the sector consolidates, the ability to attract large, strategic rounds—like Anthropic’s $450 million Series C—will become a key differentiator for firms seeking to lead the next wave of AI innovation.
